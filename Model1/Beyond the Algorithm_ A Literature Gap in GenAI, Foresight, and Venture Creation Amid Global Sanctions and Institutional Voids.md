# Beyond the Algorithm: A Literature Gap in GenAI, Foresight, and Venture Creation Amid Global Sanctions and Institutional Voids

## Assessment of Core Conceptual Linkages in Contemporary Entrepreneurship Research

The contemporary academic discourse on entrepreneurship reveals a sophisticated but fragmented understanding of the core concepts central to this inquiry: Future Consciousness, Strategic Foresight, and Digital Bricolage. While significant scholarly work exists on each concept individually, and some studies connect them in pairs, a comprehensive framework that systematically integrates all three into a coherent chain related to Venture Creation remains elusive within the provided literature. This suggests that while the foundational building blocks for such a framework exist, their synthesis is still in an embryonic stage of development. The literature demonstrates distinct streams of inquiry, each focusing on different facets of entrepreneurial cognition, strategy, and action under constraint. The most developed connection is between Strategic Foresight and Digital Bricolage, often framed as mechanisms for navigating uncertainty and leveraging limited resources in volatile environments. Another emerging link connects Entrepreneurial Cognition directly to Artificial Intelligence adoption, positioning bricolage as a form of cognitive agility [[113](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/radm.12781), [133](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/radm.12781)]. However, the integration of these concepts with the more abstract notion of "Future Consciousness" and its application to venture creation is notably absent.

Strategic Foresight has established itself as a critical capability for organizational resilience and long-term planning [[54](https://kalypso.com/viewpoints/entry/using-strategic-foresight-to-guide-longer-range-innovation-investments), [77](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/navigating-the-future-with-strategic-foresight)]. It is defined as the capacity to explore multiple plausible futures and develop strategies that remain robust across a range of scenarios [[56](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/07/strategic-foresight-future-ready-organizations-wef/)]. This practice is increasingly being integrated into public sector governance, corporate strategy, and crisis management [[14](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356145596_Supply_chain_resilience_and_business_responses_to_disruptions_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic), [28](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/11/tackling-policy-challenges-through-public-sector-innovation_49144212/052b06b7-en.pdf), [141](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/strategic-foresight-investors-smarter-way-investments-bouvier-cdt0c)]. Recent systematic syntheses confirm that foresight practices can catalyze responsible environmental action and sharpen anticipatory thinking [[26](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383529376_The_state_of_foresight_in_small_and_medium_enterprises_literature_review_and_research_agenda)]. The advent of artificial intelligence has further amplified interest in strategic foresight, with organizations using AI to scan for signals of change, analyze vast datasets, and support scenario development [[6](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-in-strategic-foresight_aa573076-en.html), [66](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388837229_Collaborative_Foresight_in_the_Age_of_AI_A_Framework_for_Evolving_Human-AI_Dynamics_in_Strategic_Decision-Making_and_Futures_Research)]. International bodies like the OECD and the World Economic Forum are actively exploring how AI can reshape anticipatory governance, releasing joint publications on the topic [[33](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287/795de142-en.pdf), [65](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-strategic-foresight-expanding-beyond-limits-global-ravi-vs-zbhuc), [88](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/12/ai-strategic-foresight-future-thinking/)]. This body of work provides a mature theoretical and practical foundation for understanding how organizations prepare for the future, but it primarily focuses on established firms and governmental bodies rather than nascent ventures created under extreme duress.

Digital Bricolage, conversely, represents a bottom-up, adaptive approach to innovation, particularly prevalent in resource-constrained settings [[4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364308078_Digital_Bricolage_Creating_a_Digital_Transformation_from_Nothing), [38](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Christian-Busch-5/publication/344271289_From_necessity_to_opportunity_Scaling_bricolage_across_resource-constrained_environments/links/5f623ef9299bf1d43c0ab13b/From-necessity-to-opportunity-Scaling-bricolage-across-resource-constrained-environments.pdf)]. Rooted in the broader concept of bricolage, which involves making do by applying available resources to new problems, digital bricolage specifically examines how entrepreneurs leverage existing digital technologies to create value and survive [[39](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/entrepreneurial-bricolage-transforming-constraints-strategic-jadhav-lzfbf), [40](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/BRICOLAGE-AS-A-PATH-TO-INNOVATION-FOR-RESOURCE-NEW-Senyard-Baker/cc56f8c115fa1d3256cddeec75a646c570a5cfc4)]. Studies show that digital bricolage fosters short-term innovation and adaptation, enhancing business model innovation through digital transformation [[2](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/13/5/355), [3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Digital-Bricolage-and-Its-Limits%3A-How-Undertake-in-Karanasios-Senyo/69996cb8fe65204f278b1bc4a31aac741d278e29)]. Empirical research highlights its positive impact on new venture growth and adaptiveness, especially in emerging economies facing persistent institutional voids and resource scarcity [[18](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370803345_The_role_of_bricolage_in_countering_resource_constraints_and_uncertainty_in_start-up_business_model_innovation), [25](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/asiapa/v37y2020i4d10.1007_s10490-019-09657-1.html)]. Scholars have developed frameworks to explore the relationship between different types of bricolage and online store performance, underscoring its practical relevance [[72](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12525-018-0302-9)]. Furthermore, digitalization is seen as a potential alternative method of organizing for firms operating in highly uncertain, resource-constrained environments, enabling internationalization strategies that might otherwise be impossible [[60](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/24/14002), [61](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357180231_The_Role_of_Digitalization_on_the_Internationalization_Strategy_of_Born-Digital_Companies)]. The concept is also expanding to include platform-based models, introducing "platform bricolage" as a novel approach for industrial digital platform innovation [[79](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400452213_Platform_Bricolage_as_an_Approach_for_Industrial_Digital_Platform-Based_Innovation), [112](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jpim.70024?campaign=wolearlyview), [159](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpim.70024)].

The intersection of these two fields—Strategic Foresight and Digital Bricolage—is where some of the most relevant connections emerge. One study explicitly examines how strategic foresight enhances digital innovation performance, mediating the effects of entrepreneurial bricolage [[13](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359443853_How_does_bricolage_release_the_tensions_in_organisational_ambidexterity_for_resource-constrained_SMEs_A_moderated_mediation_framework)]. This suggests a synergistic relationship where foresight provides the directional intent and bricolage provides the tactical means to achieve it. Leaders who exhibit strategic foresight, grit, and cognitive flexibility are seen as essential for guiding firms toward recovery and growth in turbulent times [[11](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jsc.70051)]. This aligns with the idea that foresight practitioners can act as "bricoleurs," creatively applying tools and methods to solve complex problems [[12](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1052562918766350)]. However, this linkage remains largely conceptual. There is a lack of empirical research that traces how a process of strategic foresight directly informs the specific digital bricolage actions taken by founders of new ventures. The connection appears to be one of parallel capabilities rather than a sequential or causal pathway.

The most nascent and least empirically grounded concept in this triad is Future Consciousness. This term is primarily explored through psychological scales designed to measure an individual's prospective attitude [[21](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Sanna-Ahvenharju-2010238389), [28](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/11/tackling-policy-challenges-through-public-sector-innovation_49144212/052b06b7-en.pdf)]. It is linked to foresight and policy-making, suggesting that fostering a sense of future consciousness should shape decisions about public research investments [[27](https://www.academia.edu/3110447/13_LESSONS_FROM_TECHNOLOGY_FORESIGHT_STUDY_IN)]. In the context of entrepreneurship, it is mentioned as a component of sustainable entrepreneurship, alongside innovation and strategic foresight [[22](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10843-025-00387-8.pdf)]. However, the provided sources contain no studies that operationalize Future Consciousness as a driver of venture creation, nor do they connect it to the practical application of either strategic foresight or digital bricolage. It remains a high-level, almost philosophical construct, disconnected from the tangible, day-to-day problem-solving activities of entrepreneurs. The absence of this concept in empirical studies of venture creation is a significant gap, as it leaves unexamined the role of the founder's own mindset and anticipatory orientation in shaping the venture's trajectory.

## The Critical Gap in Geopolitical and Institutional Contexts

A profound and defining characteristic of the provided academic corpus is the near-total absence of research that situates the interplay between foresight, bricolage, and venture creation within the specific, high-stakes geopolitical and institutional contexts specified by the user. The literature on entrepreneurship in challenging environments overwhelmingly centers on *bricolage* and *institutional voids*, but it fails to incorporate the other key concepts of *strategic foresight* and *future consciousness*. Conversely, the burgeoning field of strategic foresight, particularly in relation to technology, operates almost exclusively within stable, high-tech economies and does not address the realities of sanctioned or isolated markets. This creates a stark dichotomy in research focus, effectively creating two parallel universes of knowledge: one concerned with overcoming systemic disadvantages through resourcefulness, and another concerned with integrating advanced technologies into established strategic processes. The user's query attempts to bridge this divide, and the lack of articles meeting all criteria confirms that this synthesis has not yet occurred in the academic record.

The body of work addressing entrepreneurship in emerging economies is heavily reliant on the concept of institutional voids—the absence of common institutional structures found in developed markets, such as reliable legal systems, efficient capital markets, and standardized regulatory frameworks [[24](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/kpm.70075), [53](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259863095_Entrepreneurship_in_Emerging_Markets_Strategies_for_New_Venture_Creation_in_Uncertain_Institutional_Contexts)]. Entrepreneurs operating in these environments are often described as filling these voids through innovative means [[104](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324843962_Who_Fills_Institutional_Voids_Entrepreneurs'_Utilization_of_Political_and_Family_Ties_in_Emerging_Markets), [106](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344458112_Institutional_Voids_Economic_Adversity_and_Inter-firm_Cooperation_in_an_Emerging_Market_The_Mediating_Role_of_Government_RD_Support)]. Research in this area frequently employs case studies from countries like India, China, and Morocco to illustrate how startups survive and grow despite these challenges [[23](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400578867_Entrepreneurial_Bricolage_in_the_Face_of_Institutional_Voids_The_Case_of_Moroccan_Startups), [40](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/BRICOLAGE-AS-A-PATH-TO-INNOVATION-FOR-RESOURCE-NEW-Senyard-Baker/cc56f8c115fa1d3256cddeec75a646c570a5cfc4), [57](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2199853125001076)]. The dominant theme is adaptation and survival through "institutional bricolage," where entrepreneurs creatively navigate and overcome the deficiencies of their institutional context [[70](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08985626.2024.2303426)]. Some scholars even identify "institutional void entrepreneurship" as a distinct phenomenon, where entrepreneurs deliberately target states with severe institutional deficits [[55](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=1187157)]. However, this entire stream of research, while conceptually rich, is fundamentally disconnected from the topics of strategic foresight and future consciousness. The entrepreneur's primary challenge is immediate and concrete—how to secure resources, navigate corruption [[130](https://intech-files.s3.amazonaws.com/a043Y000010Jz8gQAC/0015423_Authors_Book%20%282024-07-25%2010%3A08%3A46%29.pdf)], and build a customer base in an unstable environment—which leaves little room for the metacognitive practices of future consciousness or the deliberate, structured process of strategic foresight.

The second critical contextual filter, sanctioned or isolated markets, represents an even deeper chasm in the literature. Based on the provided sources, there is virtually no peer-reviewed research on venture creation in markets like Iran or Russia. The only tangential reference comes from a single source discussing Russia's use of "bricolage tactics" in a geopolitical sense, analyzing how the country deploys improvisational strategies to disrupt Western institutions [[35](https://www.iss.europa.eu/publications/briefs/deconstructing-russias-bricolage-tactics)]. This is a macro-level analysis of statecraft, not an examination of small business formation or entrepreneurship. The unique challenges posed by sanctions—including restricted access to global financial systems, international supply chains, and advanced technologies—are not addressed in any of the reviewed materials. The literature on digital entrepreneurship in emerging economies, for example, discusses connectivity facilitated by digital bricolage [[59](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00472778.2025.2569591)], but it does not consider the scenario where that connectivity is intentionally severed or severely degraded by external political forces. Consequently, the entire nexus of venture creation, digital bricolage, and strategic foresight within these specific high-risk environments remains a completely unexplored territory.

This research gap is further illuminated by comparing the focus of different academic communities. As previously noted, scholars in stable, high-tech economies are engaged in top-down inquiries about the implications of new technologies like Generative AI, focusing on questions of return on investment, ethics, and governance [[8](https://www.fudan.edu.cn/en/2026/0515/c344a149135/page.htm), [89](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/business-strategy-buried-ai-consciousness-research-marc-stoiber-9osec), [111](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-026-06301-z)]. Their world is one of abundance, and their concern is with managing the risks and responsibilities of powerful new tools. In contrast, scholars working on entrepreneurship in emerging economies are engaged in bottom-up inquiries into survival and adaptation [[38](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Christian-Busch-5/publication/344271289_From_necessity_to_opportunity_Scaling_bricolage_across_resource-constrained_environments/links/5f623ef9299bf1d43c0ab13b/From-necessity-to-opportunity-Scaling-bricolage-across-resource-constrained-environments.pdf), [57](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2199853125001076)]. Their world is one of scarcity, and their focus is on how to build something from nothing [[17](https://journal.psych.ac.cn/xlkxjz/CN/10.3724/SP.J.1042.2024.00568)]. The user's research goal sits at the precise point where these two worlds collide. It asks whether entrepreneurs in environments of geopolitical isolation and institutional decay can leverage the very same technological tools (GenAI) that are the subject of abstract debate in the West. The failure to find articles meeting these criteria strongly suggests that this question has not yet been formally posed in the academic literature.

## The Emerging Role of Generative AI and its Disconnect from Practical Application

The period encompassing 2024-2026 marks the beginning of a significant shift in how scholars and practitioners view the role of artificial intelligence in strategic and entrepreneurial domains. The provided literature reflects a growing body of work examining the impact of Generative AI (GenAI), though this research is characterized by a notable disconnect from the practical realities of venture creation in constrained environments. The discourse is bifurcated: one stream explores GenAI's potential to augment and automate strategic foresight, while another investigates its influence on entrepreneurial ideation and business model innovation. However, neither of these streams intersects with the crucial contextual factors of sanctioned markets, institutional voids, or the specific methodology of digital bricolage. Furthermore, the question of GenAI accessibility in the very contexts where it might be most needed is entirely unaddressed.

The integration of AI into strategic foresight is a prominent theme, driven by the recognition that GenAI can process real-time data from global markets, weather patterns, and socio-political developments to enhance strategic foresight capabilities [[90](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517725001554)]. Reports from the OECD and the World Economic Forum highlight that many foresight professionals are already using AI for tasks like scanning for signals of change and supporting scenario development [[6](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-in-strategic-foresight_aa573076-en.html), [65](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-strategic-foresight-expanding-beyond-limits-global-ravi-vs-zbhuc)]. This line of inquiry frames GenAI not as a competitor to human foresight but as a powerful tool to expand its limits [[66](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388837229_Collaborative_Foresight_in_the_Age_of_AI_A_Framework_for_Evolving_Human-AI_Dynamics_in_Strategic_Decision-Making_and_Futures_Research)]. Studies are investigating how GenAI can be used for strategy ideation in small businesses, such as nanostores, and how it is transforming managerial foresight by altering decision-making processes [[34](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/24/11244), [125](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403930670_Generative_Artificial_Intelligence_for_Managerial_Foresight)]. This research positions GenAI as a catalyst for more sophisticated and data-driven strategic planning, applicable to large organizations and governments aiming to navigate complexity.

Separately, a distinct body of research is emerging on the direct impact of GenAI on entrepreneurship. This work explores how GenAI tools like ChatGPT are affecting everything from the creative process to ethical considerations [[98](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390515852_Generative_artificial_intelligence_ChatGPT_technology_and_its_impact_on_the_development_of_digital_entrepreneurship_skills_among_university_students), [116](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10551-026-06301-z.pdf)]. Some studies propose frameworks for understanding how GenAI can empower entrepreneurs by enhancing creativity and ideation, while also potentially entrapping them through issues like reduced collective diversity or ambiguous authorship [[115](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joms.70011?af=R), [138](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403182412_Generative_Artificial_Intelligence_and_Ethicality_in_Entrepreneurs'_Creativity), [162](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403420864_Generative_AI_Use_in_Entrepreneurship_An_Integrative_Review_and_an_Empowerment-Entrapment_Framework)]. Other research focuses on GenAI's role in driving iterative innovation in business models and its potential for responsible integration into organizational learning [[76](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0268401226000010), [139](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/14/2/212)]. Strategic Foresight is a well-established discipline focused on organizational and policy-level planning, with recent work centering on its augmentation by AI [[6](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-in-strategic-foresight_aa573076-en.html), [66](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388837229_Collaborative_Foresight_in_the_Age_of_AI_A_Framework_for_Evolving_Human-AI_Dynamics_in_Strategic_Decision-Making_and_Futures_Research)]. Digital Bricolage is a vibrant area of study rooted in the micro-level, adaptive strategies of entrepreneurs in resource-scarce environments, primarily in emerging economies [[25](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/asiapa/v37y2020i4d10.1007_s10490-019-09657-1.html), [38](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Christian-Busch-5/publication/344271289_From_necessity_to_opportunity_Scaling_bricolage_across_resource-constrained_environments/links/5f623ef9299bf1d43c0ab13b/From-necessity-to-opportunity-Scaling-bricolage-across-resource-constrained-environments.pdf)].
