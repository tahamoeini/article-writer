## Theoretical Foundations of Entrepreneurial Cognition, Dynamic Capabilities, and Strategic Adaptation

To construct a meaningful inquiry into the relationship between entrepreneurial cognition, dynamic capabilities, and strategic adaptation, it is essential first to establish a firm grounding in the established theoretical literature. The provided sources offer a rich tapestry of definitions, interconnections, and empirical validations for these core concepts, which together form the bedrock of the proposed research model. These constructs represent different levels of analysis—from the individual mindset to the organizational capacity and finally to the firm's performance outcome—providing a multi-layered lens through which to view entrepreneurial phenomena.

**Entrepreneurial Cognition**, at its core, refers to the psychological processes and mental frameworks that entrepreneurs use to perceive, interpret, and act upon opportunities and threats in their environment [[31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888603/)]. This field of study moves beyond viewing entrepreneurship as a simple response to market stimuli, instead focusing on the "why" behind entrepreneurial behavior—the underlying cognitive mechanisms that drive decision-making [[29](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344168385_Dynamic_entrepreneurial_cognition_Current_trends_and_future_opportunities)]. Cognitive entrepreneurship posits that opportunity evaluation is not a passive reception of information but an active process where individuals encode and interpret environmental signals through the lens of their existing knowledge structures, schemas, and heuristics [[31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888603/)]. This perspective emphasizes that two entrepreneurs facing the same situation may perceive vastly different opportunities because of their distinct cognitive makeup [[20](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/6/3530)]. Recent scholarship further refines this concept, highlighting the role of intuition and judgment, suggesting that these are not opposites of analytical thought but are deeply intertwined [[59](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/claudiofeser_when-ai-does-the-work-judgment-becomes-the-activity-7436335358394511360-0d-j)]. The integration of artificial intelligence is seen as profoundly impacting this domain, not just by providing new data, but by reshaping the very epistemic landscape in which judgment is formed [[59](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/claudiofeser_when-ai-does-the-work-judgment-becomes-the-activity-7436335358394511360-0d-j)]. Entrepreneurial practice itself, characterized by experiential learning and "learning by doing," serves to strengthen and refine these cognitive patterns over time [[160](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385911422_Artificial_Intelligence_and_Strategic_Decision-Making_Evidence_from_Entrepreneurs_and_Investors)]. Therefore, entrepreneurial cognition is not a static trait but a dynamic and evolving system of knowledge and perception that fundamentally shapes how entrepreneurs sense their environment.

**Dynamic Capabilities**, in contrast, operates at the organizational level, explaining how firms sustain competitive advantage in rapidly changing environments [[115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13087567/)]. Rooted in resource-based theory, the concept, famously articulated by Teece, Pisano, and Shuen, defines dynamic capabilities as the firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competencies to address rapidly changing environments [[19](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323184001_Entrepreneurial_cognition_Exploring_the_mindset_of_entrepreneurs), [23](https://www.scribd.com/document/400554945/Entrepreneurial-Cognition-Exploring-the-Mindset-of-Entrepreneurs)]. This process is typically broken down into three stages: sensing opportunities and threats in the environment, seizing promising opportunities through resource deployment, and reconfiguring existing assets and competencies to create new value [[117](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286722178_Dynamic_capabilities_expert_and_entrepreneurial_learning)]. This framework provides a powerful explanation for strategic thinking and entrepreneurial orientation as higher-order attributes that enable firms to navigate complexity [[115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13087567/)]. The literature indicates that dynamic capabilities are not monolithic; they can be decomposed into specific components such as digital orientation, digital capability, and a willingness to change, particularly in the context of digital innovation [[10](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23299460.2025.2500154), [12](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391600486_Dynamic_capabilities_and_digital_innovation_pathways_to_competitive_advantage_through_responsible_innovation)]. Furthermore, dynamic capabilities are not inherent to a firm but are developed and deployed through managerial action and strategic initiatives [[15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13731-025-00570-0), [129](https://www.scribd.com/document/670068924/asset-orchestration-dynamic-capabilities-1)]. There is also a growing body of work extending this theory to incorporate AI as a critical innovation resource, especially in resource-constrained settings, and conceptualizing advanced AI adoption as a form of higher-order dynamic capability itself [[11](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S001985012600012X), [86](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-06851-7)]. This blurs the line between a tool and a core competency, suggesting that proficiency in leveraging AI becomes a defining feature of a firm's adaptive capacity.

The connection between these two concepts is bidirectional and symbiotic. Managerial cognition serves as a critical micro-foundation for the development of dynamic capabilities [[127](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276052175_ANTECEDENTS_TO_DYNAMIC_MANAGERIAL_CAPABILITIESCOGNITION_CORPORATE_ENTREPRENEURIAL_ACTION)]. The way managers perceive and interpret information (cognition) directly influences their ability to sense environmental changes and seize opportunities (dynamic capabilities). Studies have empirically validated the relationship between managerial cognition and the development of dynamic capabilities, showing that certain cognitive skills are instrumental in navigating organizational change [[127](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276052175_ANTECEDENTS_TO_DYNAMIC_MANAGERIAL_CAPABILITIESCOGNITION_CORPORATE_ENTREPRENEURIAL_ACTION), [128](https://www.academia.edu/37001225/Dynamic_Capabilities_and_Entrepreneurial_Ecosystems_The_Micro_foundations_of_Regional_Entrepreneurship)]. Conversely, the successful deployment of dynamic capabilities creates an organizational context that reinforces and shapes managerial cognition. As firms learn and adapt through experience, their collective knowledge base expands, influencing the cognitive schemas of their leaders [[118](https://hal.science/hal-01274005/document)]. This interplay is central to achieving superior international performance and fostering innovation [[83](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jinten/v13y2015i3p303-336.html), [124](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09713557251358420)]. The development of dynamic capabilities is thus contingent on the cognitive abilities of the leadership team to envision new possibilities and orchestrate the necessary resource reconfigurations.

Finally, **Strategic Adaptation** represents the ultimate outcome of this interplay. It is the firm's successful adjustment to a turbulent environment, leading to enhanced performance and resilience [[116](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392123028_Strategic_Adaptation_in_a_Dynamic_Global_Environment_Unleashing_the_Power_of_Dynamic_Capabilities_for_International_Organizational_Performance), [141](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S277303282500029X)]. This concept is synonymous with strategic change, organizational learning, and the ability to pivot business models in response to market shifts or crises [[117](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286722178_Dynamic_capabilities_expert_and_entrepreneurial_learning), [161](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bsd2.70344)]. In the contemporary context, strategic adaptation is increasingly driven by technological innovation, particularly in AI [[97](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/23/10494)]. Firms leverage their dynamic capabilities to translate technological advancements into tangible benefits, such as improved operational resilience, new product development, and enhanced service innovation [[14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12469106/), [141](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S277303282500029X)]. The process involves a range of strategic responses, including market hedging, pivoting, or even exiting non-viable ventures [[162](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969593126000405)]. The goal is to achieve a superior fit between the organization and its environment, thereby sustaining a competitive advantage. The literature consistently links the development of dynamic capabilities directly to the ability to engage in strategic adaptation, positioning dynamic capabilities as the engine that powers a firm's journey from sensing an opportunity to successfully adapting its strategy to capitalize on it [[15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13731-025-00570-0), [116](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392123028_Strategic_Adaptation_in_a_Dynamic_Global_Environment_Unleashing_the_Power_of_Dynamic_Capabilities_for_International_Organizational_Performance)]. The proficiency of managerial technology skills is noted as playing a crucial role in implementing Generative AI to build entrepreneurial resilience during crises, underscoring the importance of cognitive alignment with technological capability [[50](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166497224001135)].

In summary, the theoretical foundation rests on a clear, logical progression: an entrepreneur's cognitive framework (Entrepreneurial Cognition) enables them to perceive and interpret environmental cues. This perception drives the development of organizational processes and competencies (Dynamic Capabilities) aimed at exploiting those cues. The successful execution of these capabilities leads to adjustments in the firm's strategies and structure (Strategic Adaptation), allowing it to thrive in a dynamic world. This established triad provides the perfect scaffolding upon which to build a more nuanced investigation incorporating the complexities introduced by generative AI and its surrounding institutional context.

## Generative AI Dependency and Its Implications for Entrepreneurial Judgment

The advent of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) introduces a profound and multifaceted challenge to the established relationships within the entrepreneurial cognition-dynamic capabilities-strategic adaptation model. GenAI is not merely another technological tool; it acts as an external agent that actively reshapes the cognitive and operational landscapes of firms. The concept of "generative AI dependency" encapsulates this deepening integration, where entrepreneurs and organizations come to rely on AI systems for tasks ranging from creative ideation and content generation to complex data analysis and strategic forecasting [[62](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1472811726000170), [135](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401195037_Artificial_Intelligence_as_a_Strategic_Tool_for_Entrepreneurial_Decision-Making)]. This dependency creates a complex dynamic that simultaneously enhances cognitive and analytical capabilities while posing significant risks to human judgment and skill retention [[36](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43621-025-01094-z), [135](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401195037_Artificial_Intelligence_as_a_Strategic_Tool_for_Entrepreneurial_Decision-Making)].

From one perspective, GenAI functions as a powerful augmentative tool for entrepreneurial cognition. It can enhance intuitive decision-making by processing vast datasets far beyond human capacity, uncovering hidden patterns, and generating analytically rigorous options that entrepreneurs can then evaluate with their unique contextual understanding [[37](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9709/12/4/135), [57](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X25003902)]. This synergy allows for decisions that are both data-driven and imbued with human insight, potentially leading to more robust strategic choices [[57](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X25003902)]. Studies have explored how GenAI is being incorporated into entrepreneurship education to foster e-entrepreneurial self-efficacy and how it transforms the landscape of innovation and ethical practices [[88](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382509432_From_mindset_to_market_unveiling_the_nexus_of_cognition_behavior_and_environment_in_igniting_students'_E-entrepreneurial_intentions), [121](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401377767_Multiple_pathways_to_generative_AI_entrepreneurial_behavior_A_mixed-methods_study_of_Chinese_undergraduates)]. By automating routine tasks, GenAI frees up managerial cognitive resources, allowing founders to focus on higher-order strategic concerns like vision setting, stakeholder management, and creative problem-solving [[140](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15411v3)]. This augmentation is central to building entrepreneurial resilience, particularly during crises, by enabling firms to respond faster and more effectively to disruptions [[50](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166497224001135), [161](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bsd2.70344)]. The OECD has reviewed experimental research indicating that GenAI has a significant impact on productivity, innovation, and entrepreneurship, reinforcing its role as a catalyst for economic activity [[65](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/the-effects-of-generative-ai-on-productivity-innovation-and-entrepreneurship_da1d085d/b21df222-en.pdf)].

However, this dependency carries substantial risks, chief among them being the phenomenon of over-reliance, which can lead to the erosion of essential human skills [[36](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43621-025-01094-z)]. When entrepreneurs uncritically accept AI-generated outputs, they risk outsourcing their judgment to a system whose reasoning may be opaque, biased, or simply incorrect due to "hallucinations" [[159](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-024-00316-7)]. This can negatively impact critical thinking and problem-solving abilities, creating a cognitive dependency that diminishes human agency and expertise [[36](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43621-025-01094-z), [40](https://www.scribd.com/document/834170642/002-AI-Book-in-Honour-of-Prof-Abubakar-Adamu-Rasheed-Volume-3-Draft-Edition)]. Research highlights that over-reliance is a persistent behavioral issue; users often maintain high levels of reliance on automated advice even when the AI's accuracy declines, demonstrating a failure to appropriately calibrate trust in the system [[152](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686164), [154](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382836608_Understanding_Trust_and_Reliance_Development_in_AI_Advice_Assessing_Model_Accuracy_Model_Explanations_and_Experiences_from_Previous_Interactions)]. This erosion of skill is not just a theoretical concern; it has practical implications for ethical standards, as AI reliance could lead to a decline in ethical rigor if entrepreneurs delegate moral judgments to algorithms without sufficient oversight [[40](https://www.scribd.com/document/834170642/002-AI-Book-in-Honour-of-Prof-Abubakar-Adamu-Rasheed-Volume-3-Draft-Edition)]. The very nature of GenAI's reliance on existing data means that while it can contribute to creative ideas, it may also perpetuate existing biases and limit true novelty if not guided by human creativity [[38](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385215972_The_Impact_of_Generative_Artificial_Intelligence_on_Ideation_and_the_performance_of_Innovation_Teams_Preprint)].

This tension between augmentation and substitution forms the crux of the challenge posed by GenAI dependency. It forces a re-evaluation of the role of the entrepreneur. If AI can generate analyses, summaries, and even strategic models, the entrepreneur's primary function shifts from information processing to judgment [[59](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/claudiofeser_when-ai-does-the-work-judgment-becomes-the-activity-7436335358394511360-0d-j)]. Judgment becomes the key asset, involving the ability to critically evaluate AI-generated insights, understand their limitations, and embed them into a broader strategic narrative [[59](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/claudiofeser_when-ai-does-the-work-judgment-becomes-the-activity-7436335358394511360-0d-j), [110](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amorimpedro_calibrate-ai-use-to-the-decision-at-hand-activity-7457908147119316992-Aa0x)]. This reframing positions strategic decision-making itself as a dynamic capability, one that must be continuously honed to work effectively with AI [[1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3051051126000052)]. The proficiency of managerial technology skills is therefore paramount, as leaders must not only adopt the technology but also master the art of working *with* it [[50](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166497224001135)].

For the purpose of the proposed research model, generative AI dependency should be conceptualized as the primary environmental stimulus that disrupts the equilibrium between entrepreneurial cognition and dynamic capabilities. It introduces a new variable that demands a recalibration of both individual mental models and organizational routines. The firm's response to this dependency—whether it chooses to embrace, resist, or cautiously experiment with GenAI—will determine the trajectory of its strategic adaptation. The literature strongly suggests that a naive acceptance of AI is insufficient; instead, a more sophisticated approach involving careful judgment, continuous learning, and the development of new cognitive skills (e.g., prompt engineering, critical evaluation of AI outputs) is required for sustainable success [[74](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S219985312500188X), [90](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369393158_The_Artificially_Intelligent_Entrepreneur_ChatGPT_Prompt_Engineering_and_Entrepreneurial_Rhetoric_Creation)]. Thus, generative AI dependency is not a passive backdrop but an active force that necessitates a fundamental transformation in how entrepreneurs think, how firms organize, and ultimately, how they adapt to survive and prosper.

## Navigating Uncertainty: The Role of Regulatory Sandboxes and Policy Dynamics

The integration of transformative technologies like generative AI does not occur in a vacuum; it unfolds within a complex and often uncertain institutional environment. Two critical contextual factors that shape entrepreneurial strategy and adaptation in this domain are policy uncertainty and the emergence of regulatory sandboxes. These elements act as powerful moderators, influencing the pace, direction, and feasibility of innovation. Understanding their interplay is essential for developing a complete picture of the challenges and opportunities faced by modern entrepreneurs.

**Policy uncertainty** is a pervasive theme in the provided literature, consistently identified as a major impediment to technological adoption and firm innovation [[8](https://fsi9-prod.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2024-12/GenAI_Report_REV_Master_%20as%20of%20Dec%2012.pdf), [51](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382307405_Building_entrepreneurial_resilience_during_crisis_using_generative_AI_An_empirical_study_on_SMEs)]. This uncertainty arises from the rapid pace of technological change, which often outstrips the ability of governments to formulate stable and coherent regulatory frameworks [[8](https://fsi9-prod.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2024-12/GenAI_Report_REV_Master_%20as%20of%20Dec%2012.pdf)]. When policies are ambiguous, frequently changing, or subject to political debate, they introduce significant unknown risks for businesses, making long-term investment and strategic planning difficult [[53](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/17/7817)]. Economic policy uncertainty, in particular, has been shown to negatively affect firm innovation, as frequent policy shifts create an unstable operating environment [[53](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/17/7817)]. This is especially true for technologies with broad societal implications like AI, where debates over risks and benefits are highly unsettled, casting a cloud of uncertainty over the entire policy landscape [[8](https://fsi9-prod.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2024-12/GenAI_Report_REV_Master_%20as%20of%20Dec%2012.pdf)]. This uncertainty can manifest in various forms, including ambiguity around data privacy, intellectual property rights for AI-generated content, liability for algorithmic errors, and the potential for job displacement [[44](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2199853125001428), [64](https://www.ciis.org.cn/english/ResearchOutcomes/PUBLICATIONS/202412/W020241208743034168427.pdf)]. For entrepreneurs, navigating this fog of uncertainty requires a high degree of agility and foresight, as misjudging the regulatory trajectory can lead to significant financial and reputational losses.

In response to this challenge, a novel form of "innovation-friendly" regulation has emerged: the **regulatory sandbox** [[6](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-forum-on-ai-law-and-governance/article/regulatory-sandboxes-for-ai-in-the-majority-world-a-learningcentric-approach-to-legal-adaptation/2352427E99FCEA2B34F4B8DB1DC18095)]. A regulatory sandbox is a controlled environment—a "safe space"—where innovators, particularly fintech companies, can test new products, services, and business models without immediately being subject to all the usual regulatory requirements [[13](https://www.mdpi.com/2674-1032/4/2/26), [134](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377985881_Advances_in_Emerging_Financial_Technology_and_Digital_Money)]. This approach allows regulators and firms to collaborate in real-time, identifying potential issues and co-designing solutions before full-scale market launch [[134](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377985881_Advances_in_Emerging_Financial_Technology_and_Digital_Money)]. The sandbox concept is explicitly designed to reduce policy uncertainty by providing clarity and predictability during the critical early stages of innovation [[93](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367042023_The_sandbox_approach_to_FinTech_regulation_a_case_study_of_China)]. By permitting trial periods, often with relaxed compliance rules, sandboxes lower the barriers to entry for startups and allow for iterative learning that would be impossible in a rigid, prescriptive regulatory regime [[42](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24051v1), [47](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3745238.3745374)]. The effectiveness of this approach has been demonstrated in case studies of the UK's FinTech Regulatory Sandbox, which facilitated market access and innovation [[13](https://www.mdpi.com/2674-1032/4/2/26), [134](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377985881_Advances_in_Emerging_Financial_Technology_and_Digital_Money)].

The significance of regulatory sandboxes for the proposed research model is twofold. First, they represent a tangible institutional mechanism that directly addresses the challenge of policy uncertainty. They provide a practical pathway for firms to engage with disruptive technologies like generative AI in a structured manner. Second, and more analytically, participation in a regulatory sandbox can be conceptualized as the deployment of a specific type of dynamic capability—one related to governance and institutional navigation. Successfully leveraging a sandbox requires firms to develop the ability to sense regulatory intentions, seize opportunities for collaboration with authorities, and reconfigure their projects to meet sandbox-specific requirements. This "governance capability" becomes a critical enabler of strategic adaptation in an era of adaptive governance [[17](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/government-public-sector-services/government-trends/2026/future-of-regulation.html), [63](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04554v1)]. The establishment of at least one AI regulatory sandbox in each EU Member State, as mandated by the EU's AI Act, underscores the growing recognition of this tool's importance in fostering responsible AI innovation [[48](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/progress-in-implementing-the-european-union-coordinated-plan-on-artificial-intelligence-volume-1_533c355d-en/full-report/build-strategic-leadership-in-high-impact-sectors_35869079.html), [120](https://academic.oup.com/policyandsociety/article/44/1/38/7965776)]. Similarly, China implemented a "regulatory sandbox" mechanism to allow digital platform enterprises to conduct pilot businesses, demonstrating the global adoption of this approach [[92](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-06811-1)]. For entrepreneurs, the sandbox is not just a testing ground but a strategic tool for managing uncertainty and accelerating the development of their core dynamic capabilities in a high-stakes environment.

## AI Judgment Calibration as a Mediating Mechanism

While generative AI dependency presents a challenge to entrepreneurial cognition and policy uncertainty creates friction for strategic adaptation, a critical mediating process emerges to bridge these gaps: **AI judgment calibration**. This concept moves beyond the simplistic dichotomy of either blindly trusting or entirely rejecting AI outputs. Instead, it focuses on the sophisticated, conscious effort required to balance human intuition and expertise with algorithmic assistance, ensuring that judgment remains sharp, appropriate, and ethically sound [[89](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396643818_A_cognitive_approach_to_human-AI_complementarity_in_dynamic_decision-making), [110](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amorimpedro_calibrate-ai-use-to-the-decision-at-hand-activity-7457908147119316992-Aa0x)]. This process is arguably the most novel and crucial element for understanding how entrepreneurs can successfully integrate AI into their decision-making without sacrificing their core cognitive assets.

The literature defines trust calibration as the delicate balance between human trust in an AI application and the actual trustworthiness or reliability of that application [[112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08989)]. Effective calibration ensures that entrepreneurs neither underutilize AI's capabilities due to excessive skepticism nor over-rely on them, leading to complacency and skill degradation [[155](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002206)]. Research demonstrates that this is a significant challenge; studies show that users' reliance on AI advice does not necessarily decline even when the AI's accuracy decreases, and they often over-rely on automated advice, even when it is flawed [[152](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686164), [154](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382836608_Understanding_Trust_and_Reliance_Development_in_AI_Advice_Assessing_Model_Accuracy_Model_Explanations_and_Experiences_from_Previous_Interactions)]. This tendency towards over-reliance stems from a variety of factors, including the persuasive power of algorithmic output, user traits, and the perceived complexity of verifying AI-generated information [[74](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S219985312500188X), [159](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-024-00316-7)]. The consequences of poor calibration can be severe, ranging from flawed strategic decisions to the erosion of professional skills and ethical lapses [[40](https://www.scribd.com/document/834170642/002-AI-Book-in-Honour-of-Prof-Abubakar-Adamu-Rasheed-Volume-3-Draft-Edition), [157](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/197399/WonjiDoh_Thesis_final.pdf?sequence=1)].

Conversely, effective judgment calibration is a skill that can be developed and supported through deliberate practices. The literature points to several mechanisms for achieving better calibration. One key approach is the use of explanations and transparency tools. Brief explanations about how an AI arrived at a conclusion can help users better understand its reasoning and adjust their level of trust accordingly [[153](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00140139.2026.2634115)]. Another important method is the implementation of explicit verification workflows and checks, where AI-generated outputs are systematically cross-referenced with other data sources or expert opinion [[90](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369393158_The_Artificially_Intelligent_Entrepreneur_ChatGPT_Prompt_Engineering_and_Entrepreneurial_Rhetoric_Creation)]. Training and education also play a vital role in improving AI literacy and fostering a healthy skepticism towards AI recommendations [[155](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002206)]. The goal is not to replace human judgment with AI but to create a synergistic partnership where each party leverages its respective strengths [[89](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396643818_A_cognitive_approach_to_human-AI_complementarity_in_dynamic_decision-making)]. Thriving in the AI era, therefore, means remaining human by retaining control over high-stakes decisions involving values, relationships, and trust, while strategically delegating tasks where AI excels [[110](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amorimpedro_calibrate-ai-use-to-the-decision-at-hand-activity-7457908147119316992-Aa0x)].

Within the proposed research model, AI judgment calibration serves as the pivotal mediating process connecting the three core components. The introduction of generative AI dependency creates a state of cognitive dissonance for the entrepreneur. To resolve this and proceed with strategic adaptation, the entrepreneur must engage in a continuous process of judgment calibration. This process directly involves their entrepreneurial cognition, forcing them to actively assess, question, and interpret AI-generated information. Simultaneously, it drives the development of dynamic capabilities. For example, a firm might develop a new organizational routine for vetting AI outputs, or invest in training programs to improve employees' AI literacy, both of which are forms of dynamic capability deployment. Finally, the successful calibration of judgment determines the quality of the firm's strategic adaptation. An entrepreneur who fails to calibrate their trust in AI may make a catastrophic decision based on flawed data, leading to a failed venture. Conversely, one who masters this skill can leverage AI to gain a decisive competitive advantage. Therefore, AI judgment calibration is not just a peripheral consideration but the central mechanism through which the entire model operates, determining whether the integration of generative AI leads to enhanced performance or strategic failure.
